Rulebook & Tactical Handbook

How to Play DuoOrb: Complete Illustrated Rules & Tactics

Two orbs. Twenty walls. One objective: be the first player to touch any square on the opposing baseline. Every turn presents a pivotal trade-off between advancing your own orb or lengthening your rival's route.

Fundamental Gameplay

The Four Canonical Rules

Every match follows strict, elegant geometry. Below is each canonical rule illustrated with board diagrams.

Rule One

The Objective & Starting Setup

The game is played on a 9×9 grid. Player 1 (Blue) begins on the center square of row 9 (e9). Player 2 (Red) begins on the center square of row 1 (e1). In a standard 2-player match, each player receives a reserve of 10 walls. The first player to reach any square on their opponent's starting edge wins immediately.

Blue moves upward toward row 1; Red moves downward toward row 9.

Rule Two

The Two Actions: Move or Place a Wall

Players take turns alternating. On your turn, you must perform exactly one of two choices:

  • Move your orb: Step one square horizontally or vertically (up, down, left, right). Diagonal moves are not allowed during standard steps.
  • Place a wall: Position a wall into the groove between squares. Each wall spans exactly two square lengths and can be placed horizontally or vertically. Once placed, walls are permanent and cannot be moved or removed.
Rule Three

The Jumping Rule: Straight & Diagonal Leaps

When two opposing orbs stand face-to-face on adjacent squares with no intervening wall, special leap mechanics apply:

Case A: Straight Jump

If the space behind the rival is clear, leap straight over them into the square beyond.

Case B: Diagonal Jump

Face-to-face with no wall between you? Jump straight over. Diagonal jumps are allowed only when the straight landing is blocked — by a wall, the board edge, or a third orb. Finished orbs in multiplayer modes become pass-through ghosts.

Rule Four

The Golden Rule: Complete Trapping is Strictly Forbidden

You may build sprawling labyrinths and detours that force your opponent to walk thirty steps out of their way. However, you cannot completely seal off any player. Every legal wall placement must leave at least one open path connecting each player to their respective victory edge.

Engine Enforcement: In the DuoOrb app and online arena, our graph-validation algorithm checks connectivity before allowing a wall drop. Illegal placements that create zero-path dead ends cannot be confirmed.

Match Recording & Replay Standard

How to Read DuoOrb Algebraic Notation

Every match played in DuoOrb is recorded in a compact, human-readable coordinate notation used by the move stepper and analysis engine.

1. Grid Coordinates

The 9×9 board uses columns a through i (left to right) and ranks 1 through 9. Each seat is prefixed by its color code: B (Blue, Seat 1), R (Red, Seat 2), G (Green, Seat 3), and Y (Yellow, Seat 4).

2. Orb Steps (Bd5)

An orb move combines the seat letter with the destination square. For example, Bd5 means the Blue orb stepped (or jumped) onto column d, rank 5. Similarly, Re7 records the Red orb moving to square e7.

3. Wall Drops (Hd4 / Ve6)

Wall placements sit on the 8×8 internal intersection lattice (anchored by the top-left square of the 2×2 block the wall bisects). Hd4 places a two-square Horizontal wall anchored at d4; Ve6 places a Vertical wall anchored at e6.

Instant Legality Check

Why a Wall Drop Gets Accepted or Rejected

Before any wall lands, the engine runs four checks in order. Learn them and you will never be surprised by a rejected placement — or miss a chance to punish one.

Checks 1–2: Stock and Geometry

First, you must have a wall left in reserve — empty hands mean walking only. Second, the wall must sit fully inside the 8×8 intersection lattice, spanning exactly two squares horizontally or vertically.

Checks 3–4: Conflicts and Paths

Third, the wall must not duplicate, cross, or overlap an existing wall along the same groove. Fourth, the golden path rule: after placement, every active orb must still own at least one route to its goal, verified by breadth-first search.

Ending Early

Resigning, Flagging and Multiplayer Finishes

In 1v1, resigning or running out of time hands the win to your opponent immediately. In 3–4 player games a resignation takes the worst remaining place while the rest play on, and finished orbs become pass-through ghosts. Frequently Asked Questions

Clarifications on Common Scenarios

Can I place a wall behind my own orb?

Yes. Defensive walls that shield your back line from opponent counters are completely legal, as long as an open path to your goal line remains available.

Can I pass my turn without moving or placing a wall?

No. Quoridor and DuoOrb do not permit passing. You must execute either an orb move or a legal wall placement on every turn.

What happens in 4-player games when three orbs cluster?

In 4-player mode, you can jump over one adjacent opponent. If a second opponent stands behind the first, you cannot double-jump in a straight line; you must leap diagonally to an open adjacent square.
