Rulebook & Tactical Handbook

How to play DuoOrb and Quoridor: Complete official rules

Two pieces. Twenty walls. One objective: be the first player to touch any square on the opposing baseline. Every turn presents a pivotal trade-off between advancing your own piece or lengthening your rival's route.

Fundamental Gameplay

The Four Canonical Rules

Every match follows strict, elegant geometry. Below is each canonical rule illustrated with board diagrams.

Rule One

The Objective & Starting Setup

The game is played on a 9×9 grid. Player 1 (Blue) begins on the center square of row 9. Player 2 (Red) begins on the center square of row 1. In a standard 2-player match, each player receives a reserve of 10 walls. The first player to reach any square on their opponent's starting edge wins immediately.

Blue moves upward toward row 1; Red moves downward toward row 9.

Rule Two

The Two Actions: Move or Place a Wall

Players take turns alternating. On your turn, you must perform exactly one of two choices:

  • Move your piece: Step one square horizontally or vertically (up, down, left, right). Diagonal moves are not allowed during standard steps.
  • Place a wall: Position a wall into the groove between squares. Each wall spans exactly two square lengths and can be placed horizontally or vertically. Once placed, walls are permanent and cannot be moved or removed.
Rule Three

The Jumping Rule: Straight & Diagonal Leaps

When two opposing orbs stand face-to-face on adjacent squares with no intervening wall, special leap mechanics apply:

Case A: Straight Jump

If the space behind the rival is clear, leap straight over them into the square beyond.

Case B: Diagonal Jump

If a wall or board edge blocks the straight square, leap diagonally to the left or right.

Rule Four

The Golden Rule: Complete Trapping is Strictly Forbidden

You may build sprawling labyrinths and detours that force your opponent to walk thirty steps out of their way. However, you cannot completely seal off any player. Every legal wall placement must leave at least one open path connecting each player to their respective victory edge.

Engine Enforcement: In the DuoOrb app and online arena, our graph-validation algorithm checks connectivity before allowing a wall drop. Illegal placements that create zero-path dead ends cannot be confirmed.
Tactical Wisdom

Wall Parity and the Tempo Paradox

Novice players often rush to spend their 10 walls in the opening 5 turns. This is known as the Wall Depletion Trap. When you run out of fences, your rival controls the board state completely and can shepherd you into a long labyrinth without resistance.

The Grandmaster Principle: Every wall you place should ideally accomplish two things: increase the opponent's shortest path distance by at least 2 steps, while maintaining your own escape flexibility. Always reserve 2 to 3 walls for the endgame race.

Grandmaster Analysis

How DuoOrb Analyzes Your Decisions

Just like modern chess platforms evaluate pawn structure and king safety, DuoOrb's engine evaluates path differential and tempo advantage.

Positional Path Evaluation (+0.9)

DuoOrb's analysis engine calculates the shortest distance to the goal line for both players after every turn. When a player commands a shorter path or forces a costly detour upon their opponent, the engine highlights that positional advantage on the live evaluation bar.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clarifications on Common Scenarios

Can I place a wall behind my own orb?

Yes. Defensive walls that shield your back line from opponent counters are completely legal, as long as an open path to your goal line remains available.

Can I pass my turn without moving or placing a wall?

No. Quoridor and DuoOrb do not permit passing. You must execute either an orb move or a legal wall placement on every turn.

What happens in 4-player games when three orbs cluster?

In 4-player mode, you can jump over one adjacent opponent. If a second opponent stands behind the first, you cannot double-jump in a straight line; you must leap diagonally to an open adjacent square.

Can I play against AI bots to practice these rules?
